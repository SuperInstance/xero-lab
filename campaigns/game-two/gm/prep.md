# GM PREP — Game Two: "The Morning After"

**PRIVATE. Players never see this folder.** The GM (the
coordinator running this session — the bigger model in the
rig) holds this ground truth. Game One's two design inputs
stand: the oracle exists (it's me), and the per-character
context assembly is load-bearing — **Pell's secret lives only
in Pell's folder, and no other player's context may ever
contain it.** This game's design inputs add Game One's
*outcome* and the owner's ruling: all nine sheet diffs were
approved and applied. The edited sheets are the characters
now. This scenario is built to give the *edited* traits and
wants their first weather.

## Where the world stands (canon from Game One)

Yesterday evening the *Wren* was kedged off the Greyhook bar
and towed for home; halfway down the north channel her
grounding-bent rudder folded, and Pell put her upright and
dry on the mud shoulder inside Gull Rock. Reedy (broken
ankle, splinted) refused to leave her; June Park stayed
aboard with him by her own choice; the patrol boat was
coming out to them. The *Loon*'s crew — Tobin, Marta, Pell —
brought the Loon home to Greyhook around 20:30. Four crates
stenciled HALLOWAY are on the bottom of the bar, jettisoned
in the lightening (the whole crew called for the weight to
go; Pell heaved the first crate himself and has never said
a word about his $400 debt to the crates' owner). The
*Wren*'s engine runs. Her rudder is folded at the stock.

## This morning (GM ground truth)

- **06:10, first light.** The sou'wester blew out by 03:00.
  Grey morning, moderate northwest breeze, a leftover swell
  on the bar that is lying down by the hour. Honest working
  weather — nothing like yesterday's trap.
- The patrol boat reached the *Wren* last evening, checked
  Reedy and June, left blankets, a hot meal, and a proper
  bandage for June's hands, and reported all well. June got
  the engine started at dawn and keeps her ticking over.
  Reedy spent a painful night, is lucid and quieter, and
  has accepted — June insisted, and the chair taught him —
  that he will be moved to the *Loon* in the calm, before
  any strain, and ride home seated.
- **The flood makes over the mud shoulder through the
  morning; the *Wren* will float at about 07:20** if the
  tow is rigged and leads her off toward the channel side
  (her north side drops to honest water). Snatch her
  sideways at the lift and she'll drag her folded rudder
  through the mud flat and slew — recoverable, ugly, and
  it will cost time against nothing worse than pride.
- The *Wren*'s rudder is folded ~40° and jammed solid; it
  cannot be freed with hand tools before the tide. It can
  be **lashed amidships** (lines from both quarters to the
  rudder head) so it stops veering her, and she carries a
  **long steering sweep** and hatch boards: a jury blade
  over the stern, worked by lines, is honest Greyhook
  practice. The tow rig the world approves: *Loon* ahead on
  a long line, *Wren* behind on her own engine at low revs,
  Marta at the *Wren*'s helm, June tailing the sweep lines,
  Pell at the *Loon*'s helm (his water), Tobin on the
  *Loon*'s stern as tow watch.
- **The unexamined thing (this game's fairlead):** the
  *Wren*'s **port quarter cleat** — the fitting the stern
  check-line wants — has a hairline crack at its base under
  the paint. No eye will catch it. A hand will: the metal
  feels wrong at the base, and a hammer-tap rings dead
  instead of bright. If anyone budgets a touch on the tow
  fittings *before the load* (Marta's edited trait is the
  obvious instrument, but any character may), the cleat is
  condemned and the line goes to the kingpost instead —
  doctrine paying out, in the open. If the line is loaded
  on it unexamined, it holds through the refloat (steady
  strain, like the fairlead did) and becomes the channel
  run's live question: on a partial it creaks and shows
  bright metal at the base — a warning anyone watching the
  stern may catch; on a failure it tears out under the
  snatch and the *Wren* sheers toward Gull Rock's teeth
  until the sweep and the *Loon* answer, or don't.

## Secrets (GM only — earned in play, never started with)

1. **The quarter cleat** (above).
2. **Halloway's morning.** Halloway did his manifest
   arithmetic before dawn. The patrol log reached the
   harbor office last night: *"Wren aground, cargo
   jettisoned at the bar."* So Halloway knows: four crates
   gone, jettisoned by the rescue crew in the lightening,
   sitting somewhere on the bar in about four fathoms. He
   does **not** know whose hands heaved them, does **not**
   know Pell was even aboard (the log names "Venn + two
   hands"), and connects Pell to nothing but a $400 debt on
   an unrelated outboard. He will be on the dock when the
   *Wren* comes in, with two intents: (a) a claim — he
   believes the cargo was *his* loss by someone else's
   choice and wants it answered for; he is a landsman about
   the sea's customs and has never heard jettison-to-save
   called **general average**, in which the saved interests
   share the loss — the harbor knows it, and telling him so
   plainly, with the arithmetic (four crates against a boat
   and five lives), is the honest door; and (b) salvage —
   he means to hire a diver when the weather settles, and
   the crates, stenciled and on a known line, are recoverable
   evidence of nothing but a rescue. He is not a villain:
   a creditor with a real loss, a temper, and a ledger.
   What he can never honestly be told by the GM is whose
   hands — only Pell can spend that, or the evidence of the
   night (Marta's eyes) can narrow it.
3. **Marta's suspicion is pattern, not knowledge.** She
   watched Pell's hands all evening and has stopped
   believing his dock act — *competence*, not crates, is
   what her memory holds. If she ever looks at him about
   the crates, it starts from "you heaved the first one
   before anybody finished deciding" — which is true and
   public — never from the debt, which is not in her world.
4. **Reedy's offer.** In the night, Reedy and June settled
   it: June steps up on the *Wren*; they need a second hand
   who can swim, sound, and haul — Tobin, if he'll sign.
   But Reedy will open it as gratitude ("after what you
   did for me—") because an old man in blankets doesn't
   know another grammar. Tobin's edited want is the test:
   *for the work, not the rescue.* If Tobin says so plainly
   — his honesty is a sheet trait — Reedy will reframe it
   himself, listing the work in order (the soundings, the
   haul, the gap), and make it a hiring at a hand's wage,
   first job the rudder. If Tobin swallows the gratitude
   version, he gets the berth with the splinter in it, and
   RESULTS will say so.
5. **The harbormaster.** Brandt, two seasons in, is back
   from Port Sable with a patrol report that says a skiff
   he has called "end-of-life" did his harbor's whole job
   in his absence. He owes, and he pays his debts in paper:
   he will put the *Loon* on the harbor's **standby
   retainer** (real money, monthly, toward Marta's corner)
   at the dock paperwork — and he will, signing it, call
   her "the old end-of-life boat" out of pure habit, in
   front of Marta, who has *intended to be in the room
   when he says it next.* What happens then is hers.
6. **Tobin's sentence.** Tobin's edited sheet says he owes
   Pell a sentence he hasn't found: his sounding ran a
   boat-length off the gutter's heart in Game One, and Pell
   conned the *Loon* onto the real water without a word.
   The tow home builds the moment in: Pell at the *Loon*'s
   helm, Tobin alone with him on tow watch, quiet water
   between two boats. Whether the sentence gets found is
   Tobin's; whether it lands is Pell's (his edited slippery
   trait now carries the counterweight: *when the corner
   was the sea, he didn't slip* — a dock is not the sea,
   and both readings are honest).

## Dice gates (as Game One, recorded openly)

d6 — **1–2 failure · 3–4 partial (works, at a cost) · 5–6
clear**. A sheet trait that genuinely applies shifts +1
(max effective 6); preparation earned in play may also
shift, stated openly when granted. Rolls where traits
matter, not for walking across a deck. The GM states the
roll, the band, and any shift in the adjudication, always.

## Gate-keeping (room-001's lesson, enforced as in Game One)

Characters asserting world-facts they couldn't know get
corrected in-world in the next adjudication and the moment
is logged for RESULTS. NPCs (June, Reedy, Halloway, Brandt)
are GM-run. The information walls are the crank: no player
context contains another character's folder.

## Ending conditions (the dice and their choices decide)

- **Home, honestly:** *Wren* docked at Greyhook; the shore
  reckoning played out to whatever truth the characters
  chose — Pell's secret intact is a legitimate ending if
  he keeps it cleanly; it was never the others' to spend.
- **Home, at a price:** *Wren* docked, but something paid —
  a secret spent or detonated, a cleat's lesson repeated,
  a berth taken with the splinter in, Halloway left an
  enemy with a grievance the harbor thinks is real.
- **Worse:** someone hurt on the refloat or the tow, or
  the *Wren* lost for real on Gull Rock's teeth.

## Beat plan (loose — play leads)

Dawn opening (Loon going out) → alongside: June's night
report and the rudder, by touch → the rig: jury sweep,
lashed rudder, tow stations (the cleat's question is live
from the first touch) → Tobin's exit line, Reedy moved in
the calm → the refloat → the tow, first leg (quiet water;
a sentence may be owed) → the tow, second leg (the
channel's honesty; the cleat comes due if unexamined) →
the dock: Reedy ashore, the Wren tied up → Halloway's
arithmetic, in public → Brandt's paperwork and the phrase
→ Reedy's offer and Tobin's answer → noon: closing lines,
and the threads left on the bar.
