# Pell Sutter — after Game Two ("The Morning After")

*Written by the GM from the record. The proposed diffs at
the bottom are NOT applied to his sheet — they wait for
the owner's ruling, as the last round did.*

## What happened to Pell in Game Two

He conned the Loon out at dawn, briefed the jury rig to
June like a hand who'd done it before, and took the
tow's helm: the refloat came off clean on Tobin's line,
and at the Gull Rock elbow his water-sense turned a
failure into a partial — the Wren sheered, kissed the
bank, and came home anyway. On the dock, at Halloway's
question, nobody was looking at him — and he stepped
forward anyway, hands in pockets, and said the truest
cheap version available: *I was there. I helped. It was
a crew decision.* It was also a third of the truth, and
he knows the fraction exactly. Brandt wrote general
average over the whole business, no fault found, and
Pell gave his name for the harbor book — *Pell Sutter,
Loon, crew* — and stood there while it was written,
heart pounding, and nothing happened. He counselled
Tobin, in parallel with Marta and in nearly her words,
to take the berth on his own terms. He received Tobin's
sentence at the end of the morning — the bad mark owned,
the cover named, thanks given standing up — and answered
it the only way he has: a nod, and work-words. In the
game's last minute, alone with the coiled lines, he made
a decision about Reedy's unspoken IOU: he's tired of
carrying it unspent. He hasn't said what he'll do with
it. The record closes with the intention standing.

## Scars — what the game wrote into him

- The secret survived its hardest morning — but it
  changed shape under him. His anonymity used to be
  absence: nobody knew he was there. Now it's a public
  record that he was crew, one arithmetic-step closer to
  anyone who ever adds the night up twice. He kept the
  hands anonymous and lost the alibi of not existing.
- General average forgave the money in public and left
  the authorship exactly where it was: his, private,
  compounding. He has learned that being cleared is not
  the same as being quit of a thing, and the distinction
  itches in a place his fast talk can't reach.
- He stood up when hiding was free. He doesn't know what
  to do with that about himself yet — whether it was
  loyalty, arithmetic, or the corner-not-being-the-sea
  again. The data point is in the record even if he files
  it under weather.
- Tobin's sentence landed. Somebody thanked him for the
  cover by naming it a debt and paying it — which makes
  Pell the creditor in a kindness, a position he has no
  practice holding.

## What Pell carries into Game Three

- The $400, unchanged in size and worse in company:
  month-end still coming; the crates public now, lying on
  the kedge line as a rescue's honest residue; Halloway's
  diver going down when the weather settles, to raise
  lamp oil and engine parts in front of the whole harbor —
  evidence that says *jettison*, and nothing about hands,
  unless someone asks the water the right question.
- His name in the harbor book as the Wren's rescue crew.
  A fact. Facts are what he swims in; this one he can't
  take back.
- Reedy's IOU, and his own closing decision to spend it —
  content unplayed, a door he has decided to open without
  deciding what's behind it. Whatever he does, he means
  to do it where the money can move and Halloway can't
  hear. Probably.
- A nod owed in the other direction: Tobin said his
  sentence in full sentences. Pell's half of that
  conversation — why he covered the mark, what it cost,
  what he thinks of the kid — is still unsaid, and both
  of them know it now.
