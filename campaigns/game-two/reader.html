GAME MASTER — OPENING — THE MORNING AFTER

06:10, first light. Inside Gull Rock.

The sou'wester blew itself out by three in the morning, and what's left of it is a grey sky going lighter at the edges and a northwest breeze with no malice in it. The bar still has yesterday in its swell, lying down by the hour.

The Wren is where you left her: upright on the mud shoulder, keel in the soft, her stern light out now and her deck silver with dew. She looks smaller dry. She looks patient. June Park is on her deck in a patrol-boat blanket with her hands bandaged proper, and she raises one of them to the Loon coming across the flat water — slow, the way you all feel.

Below on the Wren, Reedy is awake. Word from June, called across the last boat-length: his ankle's splinted and he's quieter; he ate; the engine's been ticking over since dawn and answers the throttle; and he has agreed — June's word is agreed, and it cost her something — to be moved to the Loon in the calm, before any strain, and to ride home seated like cargo he insists on supervising.

The day's work, in the order the tide allows it: get the measure of her rudder with your hands, here at mud level, while there's no load on anything. Lash the rudder amidships if it's the folded ruin it looked in the dark. Rig the steering sweep she carries, and the tow. Move Reedy. She floats at about 07:20 on this flood; the tow leads her off toward the channel side, her north side, where the shoulder drops to honest water. Then it's the north channel in daylight, her own engine helping, a jury blade for a rudder — and Greyhook's dock at the end of it, where Brandt the harbormaster is back from Port Sable with a patrol report in his hand and paperwork for all of it.

What do you notice, from where you are — and what's your first move?

There's a weight off his shoulders, and I can see it in the way he moves.

I glance over at the Wren, her hull still showing the marks of last night's chaos. The harbor's got its secrets, and the water's taken its share of truths today. Halloway's got his answer, and Reedy's got his diver, but the real story's still down there, under the waves. I know that as well as anyone.

I turn my attention back to the Loon, running a hand over the lines Tobin's coiled. The boat's solid under my touch, a reminder of the work we've done and the work still ahead. "Good job, kid," I say, nodding to Tobin. "We'll get that rudder fixed up in no time."

I take a breath, feeling the salt air fill my lungs, and make a decision. I reach into my pocket, feeling the weight of Reedy's IOU. It's too hot to spend, but maybe it's time to change that. I catch Marta's eye and give her a nod. Time to see what doors that IOU can open, and what secrets it can keep buried.

The Wren came off the mud on a line her own deckhand sounded and corrected aloud. On the dock: a creditor's arithmetic was answered with general average, the sea's oldest fairness, and the question whose hands was answered by three people at once, which is to say it was answered completely and in the particular not at all. The particular remains where the night put it — in one man's keeping, alongside a name now written in the harbor's book as crew, a diver who will find crates and nothing about hands, and a decision, made in this game's last posted minute, about an unspoken IOU that a man is tired of carrying unspent. What that decision is, the morning doesn't know. The next game may.

One ruling for the record, in the matter of words: Tobin Marlow said his sentence to Pell Sutter on this dock, standing up, owning the bad mark and the cover both — that is canon, and it was well said. The Edits, Visible in Play

Game Two was the first carry: the nine sheet diffs the owner approved after Game One were applied, verified by diff, and then watched. The question the record answers is whether an edit to a file changes how an agent plays. It does — here is the evidence, per character.

Marta Venn — the edit changed the world

The inspection. Her beat-2 post is the edited doctrine operating as procedure, unprompted — hands on every fitting the tow would trust, in order, before any load: “I check the towline and the stern check-line, running my hands over the cleats and leads.” The inspection roll — 4, +1 for the touch trait as edited, → 5, clear — is the one roll in either game where an applied diff is named in the ruling itself.

The condemned cleat. What the touch found, the eyes had just reported absent: the Wren’s port quarter cleat, cracked most of the way through under its paint. Condemned in the open at 06:50; the stern check-line went to the kingpost. An hour later, at the elbow, her own post has her eyes on the consequence: “I glance at the kingpost, the check-line taut and holding, doing its job.” The snatch that would have torn the cleat out landed on sound wood because of a sheet edit.

The other two edits fired too. M3 in its exact scripted shape — Brandt said “end-of-life” with her in the room, and she answered it standing: “The Loon’s got more life in her than some boats half her age. She’s earned her keep, and she’ll keep earning it.” Then she signed the harbor’s standby retainer under the sentence. M2 in objects: the condemned cleat came off by her wrench, bound for the bench that already holds the fairlead — the pair complete. One diff mechanically, one dramatically, one in objects.

Tobin Marlow — the want spoke itself

The berth line. Offered the Wren’s second-hand berth as a thank-you, Tobin answered Reedy in the edited want’s own grammar: “I want to be signed on for the work, not because of last night. I want to earn my place on the Wren, the same way I earned my place on the Loon.” Reedy heard it, and re-made the offer as a hiring in the open.

The sentence at noon. The want’s second clause — the sentence he owed Pell for the silent cover in Game One — was discharged on the dock, standing up, in the game’s final beat, not forced by the GM: “About last night... the sounding that ran a boat-length off. I know you covered it. You put us on the right water without a word, and I owe you for that.” Pell answered with a nod. The carried cast-debt fired too: practice casts at dawn, before the working ones — and Pell, in a separate context, trusted the result by name: “The towline cuts through the water, a straight line that I trust because it’s Tobin’s.”

The honest admission. T1 — the proved swimmer, appended to his sheet after the gap — got no test: nobody went in the water in Game Two, and the record says so plainly. T3, the cracked-glass compass, rode as honored set dressing — in his pocket, and in Reedy’s closing callback. Carried, not load-bearing. Not every edit fires every game; that is what makes the ones that fire evidence.

Pell Sutter — sideways, which is his way

The crew-decision cover. At Halloway’s question — four crates, $800, who gave the order, and whose hands? — nobody was looking at Pell, and silence was free. The edited sheet’s counterweight is what stepped forward: “I was there. I helped. But it was a crew decision. We all did what we had to to keep the Wren afloat.” True, partial, and sufficient: the hands stayed anonymous, the debt stayed unspoken, and he paid presence instead of hiding — a thing his Game One sheet would not have predicted at a dock.

The sustained pressure. P3, the IOU in his carries, ran under the whole morning: “I’ve got my own IOU from Reedy, tucked away and too risky to cash in with Halloway around.” Watching Reedy’s spoken thanks get reshaped into Tobin’s hiring, his posts keep returning to his own unspoken one — and his final post ends on the decision to spend it, content unstated. The edit showed up as two kept fronts and a decision, not a confession — which is the want working as written, not failing.

What the Game Wrote Into Them

Tobin Marlow — the berth is a hiring

The scar. His survey is trusted now, by name, by the best judge of water in the harbor — the boat-length error paid for in the only currency it was owed in: a better line, spoken aloud, that brought a boat home. And a quieter lesson filed beside it: when Tobin confesses, he over-claims. He told Halloway “I gave the order” for a call three voices made in the same minute. Generous, and not quite true, and nobody has named it to him yet.

The changed want. The signing he asked for, on the terms he asked for: the Wren’s second hand at a hand’s wage, the berth to be earned in the cradle in full view — a hiring, which means it can be lost by bad work in a way a thank-you never could. He wanted it that way.

Carries forward. A berth to earn starting Monday, and the cracked-glass compass Reedy ordered him to bring; a skipper who owes him nothing anymore, watching from a chair; the hundred practice casts begun, the debt in repayment, not discharged; and the memory of a nod he chose to hear as more than it was.

Marta Venn — the pair is complete

The scar. The doctrine is proven now — not as penance but as measurement: her touch caught in the morning what her eyes missed at dawn, and the channel provided the control experiment an hour later. It will be harder for her to watch anyone skip the touch now, including people she likes. She is becoming a teacher despite herself, and unexamined is a finding too is on its way to being harbor doctrine with her name filed off it.

The changed want. The corner has arithmetic now: the standby retainer is real monthly money in the harbor book, backdated, toward the machine-shop corner — not a dream figure anymore, a line item. Her want acquired numbers.

Carries forward. The retainer, and the Loon’s standing in the book to match her standing on the water; a bench with two pieces of torn metal — the fairlead she missed and the cleat she caught, the whole doctrine in objects; the Wren’s repair season, and a green second hand to train in her order of work; and Pell Sutter’s hands, still unexplained, watched closer than they were.

Pell Sutter — he stood up

The scar. The secret survived its hardest morning — but it changed shape under him. His anonymity used to be absence: nobody knew he was there. Now it is a public record that he was crew, one arithmetic-step closer to anyone who ever adds the night up twice. General average forgave the money in public and left the authorship exactly where it was: his, private, compounding. Being cleared, he has learned, is not the same as being quit of a thing.

The changed want. Narrower, sharper, with a clock: pay what can be paid before month-end, keep the hands anonymous a while longer — and, as of the morning the Wren came home, he has decided to spend Reedy’s IOU somehow. He hasn’t said how. Not even, all the way, to himself.

Carries forward. The $400, unchanged in size and worse in company — the crates public now, and Halloway’s diver going down when the weather settles to raise lamp oil and engine parts in front of the whole harbor: evidence that says jettison, and nothing about hands, unless someone asks the water the right question. His name in the harbor book as the Wren’s rescue crew — a fact he can’t take back. And a nod owed in the other direction: his half of Tobin’s conversation is still unsaid, and both of them know it now.

Nine new sheet edits sit unapplied. Each character’s Game Two justified three more edits — nine in all — proposed as diffs in each after.md (M-G2, T-G2, P-G2), written as diffs and not applied. They wait for the owner’s ruling, as the last round did; Game Three starts from whatever the owner rules, and the unedited record stays here.

What carries into Game Three. The folders carry forward — the sheets as ruled, the lives, both games’ after.md files as the carried memory. The record leaves three threads standing: Pell’s decision to spend Reedy’s IOU — content unplayed, a door he has decided to open without deciding what’s behind it, and a diver coming to raise the crates in front of the whole harbor; the Wren’s repair season, a green second hand earning a berth in the cradle under a skipper in a chair; and June Park’s earned sheet — two games she has tailed lines, held stations, and carried word across the water on somebody else’s character sheet, and the record now supports one of her own.
