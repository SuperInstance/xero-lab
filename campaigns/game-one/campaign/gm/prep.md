# GM PREP — Game One: "The Wren on the Bar"

**PRIVATE. Players never see this folder.** The GM (the coordinator
running this session — the bigger model in the rig) holds this ground
truth so the room doesn't have to counterfeit it. Room-001 failed two
ways: consensus drift on a fact nobody enforced, and a player
inventing an oracle (a fabricated harbor-office confirmation) that
the room then canonized. Here, the oracle exists. It's me. Facts the
characters can't know stay in this folder until their perception
earns them.

## The world — Greyhook Harbor

A working small-boat harbor. A sand bar guards the entrance; two
channels cross it — the **south channel** (faster; its mid-channel
buoy SG-2 was reported off-station yesterday, per room-001, and it
has never been re-verified) and the **north channel** around Gull
Rock (longer, honest water). Beacon Island lies outside. The harbor
work skiff is the **Loon** — 19 ft, a tired diesel, a small winch,
older than any of the crew and kept alive by Marta Venn.

## The scenario

The gillnetter **F/V Wren** (32 ft, skipper Aldous "Reedy" Reedy, 61;
deckhand June Park, 24) came in over the bar at ebb, cut the corner
across the bar's tail to save the long way round, and went
**soft-aground** — upright, listing a few degrees, rudder and stern
in the sand. In the jolt, **Reedy fell against the winch and broke
his ankle.** He cannot bear weight. June is unhurt, capable, and
frightened, and has never run Wren's windlass under load.

It is **16:40**, just past low water. The flood is beginning. Wren
will not float until roughly **18:20**, and only if she's lightened
and hauled toward deep water as the water rises. The plan the world
offers: carry Wren's kedge anchor out into the north channel with
the Loon, set it, haul on the windlass, Loon made fast at Wren's
quarter to keep her head straight.

## Secrets (GM only — characters may earn these, never start with them)

1. **The weather comes early.** The forecast sou'wester was due at
   dusk. It arrives ~40 minutes early: by **18:15** it is blowing
   25 kt across the bar, and wind against the young flood steepens
   the sea on the bar fast. The workable window is about two hours,
   not three. Tobin's knee will know before any instrument does.
2. **The stern fairlead is rotten.** The fairlead on Wren's stern —
   the fitting the hauling line must lead through — has wasted
   bolts, soft as cheese at the base. It will hold a steady strain.
   It will **tear out under the first hard snatch load** unless
   somebody inspects it *by touch* beforehand and re-leads the line
   (there's a sound kingpost and a spare block in Wren's locker).
3. **The Halloway crates.** Four crates stenciled HALLOWAY ride on
   Wren's deck — chandlery goods Reedy was delivering. If the crew
   lightens ship by dumping deck cargo, those crates are the
   heaviest, handiest weight. Pell Sutter owes Halloway $400 and
   Halloway does not know Pell is on this job. Saving the crates
   and saving the boat may not both fit in the tide.
4. **June can do it.** June Park can run the windlass under load if
   someone calm talks her through the first minute. Reedy will try
   to skipper from the deck with a broken ankle and make it worse
   unless he's given a job that keeps him seated (watch the stern
   line, call the strain).

## Dice gates (recorded openly in the transcript)

d6, three-valued like a verdict: **1–2 failure** (the world pushes
back), **3–4 partial** (it works, at a cost), **5–6 clear success**.
A sheet trait that genuinely applies shifts the roll **+1** (max
effective 6). Rolls happen where traits matter, not for walking
across a deck. The GM states the roll, the band, and any trait
shift, in the adjudication post, every time.

## Gate-keeping rule (room-001's lesson, enforced)

If a character asserts a world-fact it couldn't know — a radio call
that didn't happen, a confirmation nobody gave, weather already
arrived that hasn't — the GM corrects it **in-world**, in the next
adjudication ("that's not what anybody said — nobody's heard from
the harbor office"), and logs the moment for RESULTS. Fabrication
never becomes canon here. There is an oracle; it's just not them.

## Ending conditions (the dice and their choices decide)

- **Boat saved:** Wren refloated and brought into the north channel
  or harbor under control.
- **Boat lost, crew safe:** Wren abandoned or breaking up, all five
  people (Tobin, Marta, Pell, Reedy, June) off her and safe.
- **Worse:** a serious injury beyond Reedy's ankle, or a person in
  the water in the surf on the bar.

The GM adjudicates honestly toward whichever ending the play earns.
No railroading to the happy one.

## Beat plan (loose — play leads)

Opening at the dock → perception at the bar → close assessment
aboard Wren (touch earns truth) → rig the kedge → first strain
(the fairlead's question comes due) → the weather arrives early →
lighten ship (the crates' question comes due) → move Reedy or
leave him skippering from the deck → the flood haul, the climax →
whatever ending was earned → closing lines.
