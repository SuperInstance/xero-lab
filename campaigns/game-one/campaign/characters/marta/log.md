**Beat 1 — my post:**

The Wren's sitting there, stern down, like a duck with a broken leg. I can see Reedy's figure moving on deck, favoring one leg—yeah, that ankle's busted for sure. June's there too, waving an arm, probably trying to signal us. The tide's coming in, but it's sluggish, like it's still thinking about it. The swells are lazy, not much chop yet, but I know that sou'wester's gonna pick up by dusk.

I ease the throttle back a touch, bringing the Loon in slow. "Tobin," I call out, "get ready with that boathook. We'll come alongside her starboard, gentle-like. Don't want to knock her worse." I squint at the Wren's hull, checking for damage. She looks like she's holding, but I won't trust it till I get my hands on her.

I grab the radio mic, keeping one hand on the wheel. "Wren, this is the Loon. We're coming up on your starboard side. Sit tight, we'll get you off." I keep my voice steady, no use in them knowing I'm worried. My first move is to get us close enough to toss a line. After that, we'll see what she's made of.

**Beat 5 — my post:**

Alright, the fairlead's torn out, and that line's sawing at the stern rail like it's got a grudge. I can see the bolts that gave way — soft as cheese, rusted through. Not bad luck, just a bad part that never got replaced. The kedge's holding, but we need that line re-led, and fast.

First thing, I shout to Tobin, "Get that spare block from the locker and bring it aft! We'll re-lead through the kingpost." I keep my voice sharp, but I see June's white face and know she's rattled. "June, you sit tight for a minute. Catch your breath." I need her back in the game, but not if she's a step from breaking.

Next, I turn to the cargo. "Those Halloway crates," I call out, "we're tossing them. They're the heaviest, and we need the weight off." I don't like losing cargo, but it's the Wren or the crates, and I know which one I'm choosing.

Finally, Reedy. I glance at his ankle, already swelling. "Reedy, we're splinting that ankle. Then you're on the Loon. You can't run this show from a chair, and I need June focused, not tending to you." I say it plain, because there's no time for anything else.

I grab the gaff handle and start splinting, my hands moving quick but careful. The boats are shifting under me, and I can feel the storm's breath on my neck. We re-lead the line, lighten the load, and get Reedy secure. After that, it's up to the water and the weather.
