# Tobin Marlow — after Game One

*Written by the GM from the record (his log, the scene, the
dice). This is the persistence file: who he is now, before
anyone edits anything.*

## What happened to him

He went out as "the kid" and the night spent him like a
hand. His knee called the weather **early** when the
forecast said dusk, and the crew worked to his clock from
that moment (roll 6 — the room's true deadline). His
sounding found the gutter but his mark ran a boat-length
off, and Pell quietly put the *Loon* on the real water
without a word — Tobin knows, even if it was never said.
When the transfer failed he chose the sling over his own
footing and went into the gap between the hulls holding a
one-legged man, kept Reedy's head up through a set, and
came up over the rail grinning. He finished the night at
the quarter line in June's borrowed oilskin, shaking,
useful.

## Scars — what persists

- **The palms.** Rope-burned and torn from the sling and
  the quarter line; they'll heal into a working man's
  hands a season early.
- **The debt of craft.** His sounding was the one weak
  measure of the night and somebody else's hands covered
  it silently. He owes Pell a truth he hasn't said yet,
  and he owes the lead line a hundred practice casts.
- **The name.** Reedy, wrapped in blankets, asked June
  who'd held him in the water. She told him. Skippers
  remember that in this harbor.

## What he carries into the next game

- June's spare oilskin, borrowed and unreturned — he has
  to go back to the *Wren* anyway; there's a rudder to
  help with.
- A skipper's gratitude (Reedy's), unspoken and real.
- The knowledge, in his body now and not just his sheet,
  that he can hold a man in the gap and come up.
