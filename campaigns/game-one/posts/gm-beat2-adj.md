## GM — Adjudication, Beat 2

**⚄ Roll — Tobin, sounding for deep water: 3 · partial.**
There's deepening water off her stern, Tobin — your dip is
real, a gutter running away roughly north-northeast toward
the channel. But the chop is lying to your hands half the
time, and your last cast could be a boat-length off the
gutter's heart. You have a *line*, not a survey. Set a kedge
on it carelessly and it may start at the gutter's edge.

**⚄ Roll — Marta, hands on the stern gear: 1, +1 (trusts
nothing she hasn't touched) → 2 · failure.** Your hands found
plenty, Marta, and all of it true: the hauling winch is stiff
with old grease, the windlass is sound with rusty bolts, the
hull's scraped but nowhere breached. Following the stern line
aft, your knuckles crossed the fairlead — salt-caked,
ordinary-feeling — and then June's voice was calling you below
about Reedy, and the light was going, and the moment passed.
You have not condemned the stern gear. You have not cleared
it, either. It's simply still *unexamined*, the way a thing
is before it's needed.

Below: Reedy's ankle is broken, plain — purpled, swelling,
pointing a degree or two wrong. He bears it lying still and
nobody else. A gaff handle and sail ties would splint it,
when there's a minute. There isn't a spare one yet.

Pell — boats riding well, lines tended, and the southwest
sky is darkening where everyone can see it now.

**Beat 3.** The kedge goes out. The *Loon* will carry the
*Wren*'s kedge anchor and its line out along Tobin's line of
deepening water and set it, so the *Wren* can haul herself
toward deep water as the flood makes. Tobin: you're on the
*Loon*'s bow with the anchor. Pell: you're her helm in the
chop, over a bar you know better than anyone aboard. Marta:
you stay on the *Wren* — the windlass, June's first run at
it, and a skipper below who still thinks he's running this.
Post your part of the work.
