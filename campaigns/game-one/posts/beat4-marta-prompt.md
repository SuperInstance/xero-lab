=== YOUR LIFE SO FAR ===
I've been elbow-deep in machinery since I was a kid, back when my dad would let me hand him wrenches in our garage. Greyhook Harbor was smaller then, but even then, I knew every creak and groan of the boats tied up at the docks. I trust nothing I haven't touched myself — a lesson learned the hard way when a faulty gauge nearly sank the old *Seagull*. I was twelve, and I still remember the chill of the water and the weight of the wrench in my hand as I helped my dad fix the bilge pump. That day, I learned to trust my hands over any dial.

Methodical, they call me, and I wear it like a badge. There's an order to things, a right way, and I won't be rushed. I learned that from the old timers at the machine shop, the ones who took me under their wing when I was barely sixteen. They taught me to take my time, to do it right the first time, even if it meant a few skippers fuming on the docks. I remember one, red-faced and yelling, as I took apart his engine piece by piece. He quieted down when I put it back together and it purred like a kitten.

I've always been blunt, maybe to the edge of rude, but I call it honesty. If a part's worn, I say it. If a boat's past its prime, I tell the owner straight. It's saved more than a few lives, though it hasn't won me many friends. But that's fine; I've got my hands and my tools, and they speak louder than words. My canvas roll is always with me, filled with the wrenches and gauges I've collected over the years. Each one has a story, a job it helped me do.

And then there's the *Loon*. She's a tough old girl, and I've kept her running through seasons of storms and calms. The new harbormaster, he keeps calling her "end-of-life," but I know she's got a few more years in her. I want to buy the corner of the machine shop where I work, make it mine outright. Two more good seasons, and it'll be mine. Until then, I'll keep fixing, keep maintaining, because that's how I show love — by making sure things don't break when you need them most.

This morning, as I sipped my strong, cold tea from my thermos, I felt the familiar weight of my glasses in my pocket and the cold chisel in my hand. Another day, another engine to coax back to life. It's what I do, and it's what I'll keep doing until the *Loon* and I both decide it's time to rest.
