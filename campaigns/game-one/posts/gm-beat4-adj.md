## GM — Adjudication, Beat 4: the fairlead tears out

The heave comes on with the swell train, the *Wren* surges —
and the load doesn't climb, it *snatches*.

**The stern fairlead tears out of the deck.**

Not the line. Not the anchor. The fitting nobody finished
examining gives up its bolts all at once and the hauling
line comes free of its lead with a sound like a rifle shot,
whipping across the aft deck trailing the fairlead and two
feet of splintered decking.

**⚄ Roll — Marta, in the line's path: 6 · clear success.**
You were watching the line, Marta — watching it the way
you watch everything, for the first sign of wrong — and
you saw the fitting's base *start* to go a half-second
before it went. You're flat on the deck before the whip
cracks over you. Untouched. And from flat on your back you
can see the underside of the torn-out fairlead as it lands
beside you, and the truth of it is in your hands when you
pick it up: **the bolts are wasted to lace, soft as
cheese, years gone.** It held a steady strain and it would
never, ever have held that snatch. It was going to do this
on the first hard load whenever that load came. It isn't
bad luck. It was a fact, sitting in the open, waiting for a
hand that never quite got to it.

**⚄ Roll — June, at the winch: 3 · partial.** The lever
kicks as the load vanishes and the line's tail catches her
a glancing blow across the shoulder. She's down, winded,
hands rope-burned and already swelling, scared white — but
nothing's broken and she's breathing. She will need a
minute, and her nerve is spent down to a thin coin. Somebody
will have to give her a reason to get back up, or not ask
her to.

The kedge line now leads **raw over the stern rail**,
sawing at the wood with every surge, the strain gone out
of it; the *Wren*'s stern has eased back a few degrees
toward the shallows. The anchor's end never moved. The
sou'wester is here — properly here, a beat ahead of every
forecast, exactly the way a certain knee said it would be.
The sea on the bar is steepening by the minute.

There is a sound kingpost aft and a spare block in the
*Wren*'s locker. The line can be re-led, properly, by hands
that know how. And the *Wren* is still hanging heavy by her
rudder with the flood making — everything aft weighs on
her: the catch and ice below, and the deck cargo lashed
aft, **four crates stenciled HALLOWAY** among it.

**Beat 5.** Three problems, one tide: re-lead the line,
lighten the ship, and decide about Reedy — splint him and
move him while the boats still ride together, or seat him
aboard with a job that isn't skippering. Post your moves.
