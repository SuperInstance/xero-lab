# Pell Sutter — character sheet

- **Name:** Pell Sutter, 20
- **Nature / role:** The harbor kid. Grew up under these docks,
  runs errands, crews when there's pay, day-works wherever a
  boat is short a body. There is no dock, boat, or piece of
  waterfront business in Greyhook he doesn't know.
- **Traits:**
  - **Knows every dock and most secrets.** Who's feuding, who
    owes, which winch screams, which channel lies at low
    water — he has it all filed away.
  - **Fast talker.** Can borrow almost anything exactly once.
    Charm offensive is his first tool and his best one.
  - **Slippery when cornered.** Changes the subject, changes
    the plan, changes the room if he can. Debt, blame, and
    direct questions all get the same treatment.
  - **A better boat-hand than he lets on.** Competence gets
    you volunteered, so he rations it — but his hands know
    the work when nobody's asking.
- **Voice:** Quick, funny, name-dropping. The jokes get faster
  when he's scared. Underneath, he keeps accounts nobody sees.
- **Carries:** A borrowed phone (cracked screen), a multitool,
  a headlamp, exactly eleven dollars.
- **Want:** The **$400 he owes Halloway** the chandlery owner
  — for a seized-solid outboard he bought last spring to flip,
  a sure thing that wasn't — **gone**, before Halloway calls
  it in at month's end. Nobody on the waterfront knows but
  Halloway. Pell intends to keep it that way.

=== YOUR LIFE SO FAR ===
I was born in the shadow of the Greyhook docks, and I swear I learned to swim before I could walk, just to keep up with my old man. He was a deckhand, always chasing the next job, and I was his shadow. I guess that's where I picked up the knack for knowing every inch of this harbor – who was feuding, who was flush, which boats were running on fumes. It's a survival thing. You don't last long around here if you don't know where the bodies are buried.

The fast talking? Well, that's a skill I honed early. When you're always short on cash, you learn to borrow what you can, when you can. I remember the first time I sweet-talked old man Jenkins out of a coil of rope. I was twelve, and I needed it to fix up a skiff I was fixing to sell. Borrowed it, fixed the boat, sold it, and gave Jenkins a cut. He laughed and said I had a future in sales. I took that as a compliment.

The outboard motor? That was supposed to be my big break. Bought it off Halloway for a steal, seized up solid, but I was sure I could fix it. I was wrong. Now I'm $400 in the hole, and Halloway's been giving me the stink eye every time I pass his chandlery. I keep telling myself I'll figure something out before the end of the month, but it's getting tight.

As for the tools I carry – the phone's a hand-me-down, cracked screen and all, but it's got the numbers I need. The multitool's a lifesaver, and the headlamp's for those nights when I need to work under the docks without drawing attention. And the eleven dollars? That's my buffer, my safety net. It's not much, but it's enough to get me through another day.

I guess you could say I'm a better boat-hand than I let on. It's not that I can't do the work; I just don't want the attention. Competence gets you volunteered, and I've got enough on my plate without taking on extra jobs. But when push comes to shove, my hands know what to do. They always have.

So here I am, another day in Greyhook, trying to stay one step ahead of the game. It's not much, but it's home.

## GM — Opening: the call

*16:40, just past low water. Greyhook Harbor.*

The word came down the dock the way it always does — somebody's
cousin heard it off somebody's radio. The gillnetter ***Wren*** is
aground on the tail of the bar. She cut the corner coming in at
ebb and the sand took her: upright, listing a little to port,
stern down in the shallows. Her skipper, Reedy, came down against
the winch in the jolt and his ankle's broken. June Park, his hand,
is aboard and unhurt and very alone with it.

The harbormaster took the patrol boat to Port Sable this morning
and won't be back before 19:00. The harbor office radio is a
locked door until then. So it's the ***Loon*** — the harbor work
skiff, nineteen feet of tired diesel — because the Loon is what's
here, and you three are who's on her.

Marta has the helm; she was aboard anyway, elbow-deep in the
Loon's engine, and the engine is running because Marta Venn says
when it runs. Tobin's forward with the boathook. Pell got waved
aboard off the dock because three is a crew and two is an errand,
and Pell was standing there.

The flood has just begun to make. The forecast says the
sou'wester freshens by dusk — a working breeze, nothing more.
The bar is flat-ish now, the swells lifting slow over the tail
where the *Wren* sits with her stern light already lit and two
figures moving on her deck.

The Loon comes down the harbor toward her. **What do you notice,
from where you are — and what's your first move?**
