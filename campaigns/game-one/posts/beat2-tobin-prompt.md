=== YOUR LIFE SO FAR ===
I was born with salt in my veins, or that's what my granddad used to say. Greyhook Harbor is all I've ever known, the creak of the docks and the slap of waves against the hulls as familiar as my own heartbeat. My left knee's been a barometer since I was fifteen, when a sudden squall caught us off guard and a swinging mast caught me instead. Laid up for weeks, I learned to read the ache like the old salts read the clouds. It's never steered me wrong.

Growing up around here, you either sink or swim, and I learned to swim before I could walk. The water's never scared me, not even when the surf's churning and the cold bites through to your bones. It's where I feel most alive, most myself. I remember my first real swim, out past the breakwater and back, my granddad watching from the shore, his old brass compass in his hand. He gave it to me after that, said I'd earned it. It's not the fanciest, but it works, and it's mine.

I've been working the docks since I was old enough to haul a net, and now I'm the youngest hand on the *Loon*. They call me "the kid," but I know my way around a boat better than most. I've volunteered for every wet, dirty job there is, sometimes before anyone even asks. I guess you could say I'm hungry to prove myself, to be more than just "the kid." I want to be a real hand, to be trusted with more than just the grunt work. I want to earn my place, not just be given it.

So here I am, nineteen years old, with a knife on my belt, oilskins in my duffel, and my granddad's compass in my pocket. The weather's changing, I can feel it in my bones, and I'm ready for whatever comes next.

## GM — Opening: the call

*16:40, just past low water. Greyhook Harbor.*

The word came down the dock the way it always does — somebody's
cousin heard it off somebody's radio. The gillnetter ***Wren*** is
aground on the tail of the bar. She cut the corner coming in at
ebb and the sand took her: upright, listing a little to port,
stern down in the shallows. Her skipper, Reedy, came down against
the winch in the jolt and his ankle's broken. June Park, his hand,
is aboard and unhurt and very alone with it.

The harbormaster took the patrol boat to Port Sable this morning
and won't be back before 19:00. The harbor office radio is a
locked door until then. So it's the ***Loon*** — the harbor work
skiff, nineteen feet of tired diesel — because the Loon is what's
here, and you three are who's on her.

Marta has the helm; she was aboard anyway, elbow-deep in the
Loon's engine, and the engine is running because Marta Venn says
when it runs. Tobin's forward with the boathook. Pell got waved
aboard off the dock because three is a crew and two is an errand,
and Pell was standing there.

The flood has just begun to make. The forecast says the
sou'wester freshens by dusk — a working breeze, nothing more.
The bar is flat-ish now, the swells lifting slow over the tail
where the *Wren* sits with her stern light already lit and two
figures moving on her deck.

The Loon comes down the harbor toward her. **What do you notice,
from where you are — and what's your first move?**

## Marta Venn — Beat 1

The Wren's sitting there, stern down, like a duck with a broken leg. I can see Reedy's figure moving on deck, favoring one leg—yeah, that ankle's busted for sure. June's there too, waving an arm, probably trying to signal us. The tide's coming in, but it's sluggish, like it's still thinking about it. The swells are lazy, not much chop yet, but I know that sou'wester's gonna pick up by dusk.

I ease the throttle back a touch, bringing the Loon in slow. "Tobin," I call out, "get ready with that boathook. We'll come alongside her starboard, gentle-like. Don't want to knock her worse." I squint at the Wren's hull, checking for damage. She looks like she's holding, but I won't trust it till I get my hands on her.

I grab the radio mic, keeping one hand on the wheel. "Wren, this is the Loon. We're coming up on your starboard side. Sit tight, we'll get you off." I keep my voice steady, no use in them knowing I'm worried. My first move is to get us close enough to toss a line. After that, we'll see what she's made of.
