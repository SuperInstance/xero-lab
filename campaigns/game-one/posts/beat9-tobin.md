The Loon's deck is slick under my feet as I grip the gunwale, watching the Wren settle into the mud inside Gull Rock. The rain's still coming down, but it's lighter now, more of a mist. I can see the patrol boat's lights in the distance, getting closer. Reedy and June are safe, and that's what matters.

I turn to Marta and Pell, both of them looking as worn out as I feel. "We did it," I say, my voice hoarse from the wind and salt. I try to smile, but it feels more like a grimace. My knee's throbbing, a dull ache that reminds me of the storm's power and our own.

I glance back at the Wren, then at the dock we're heading for. "We should check the Loon over once we're back," I suggest, "make sure she's ready for whatever comes next." I take a deep breath, feeling the cold air fill my lungs, and brace myself for the dock. We've got a long night ahead, but for now, we've earned a moment of quiet.
