## GM — Adjudication, Beat 7: she floats

For the record of who stood where: Marta called Tobin to
the *Loon*, but the scramble had already decided otherwise
— Pell was over the rail and gone to her, Tobin was wanted
at the quarter line on the *Wren*'s own deck, and the
*Wren*'s quarter is where the *Loon* now lies. The crew
that took stations is the crew that posted: **Marta on the
winch, June tailing beside her; Tobin on the quarter line
and the stern watch; Pell on the *Loon*, at her quarter,
engine running.** It is not the crew Marta ordered. It is
the crew that works.

Marta waits for the swell she wants. It comes.

**⚄ Roll — the heave: 5, +1 (every preparation
succeeded — the set, the re-lead, the lightening) → 6 ·
clear success.**

The winch takes the strain slow and even. The kedge, down
in the heart of the gutter, does not drag a single inch.
The line through the kingpost sings — and holds. And the
*Wren*, lightened, hauled, and lifted by the young flood
all at once, does the thing she has been waiting three
hours of tide to do: **she floats.** A grind, a shudder
along her keel, a long second where she is neither aground
nor free — and then she is *moving*, stern-first toward
deep water, wallowing, upright, **alive**, with Tobin
paying out the quarter line hand over hand and Pell's
*Loon* taking her weight at the quarter and keeping her
head straight for the channel.

She is off the bar. She is not home.

Her engine ticks over — it ran all along, June said so,
and it answers now. Rig the tow: the *Loon* ahead, the
*Wren* behind on a long line, her own engine helping at
low revs, Marta at her helm, and the north channel in the
dark and the rain between this crew and the dock.

**Beat 8.** The run in. Twenty minutes of black water,
a rising wind astern, and one tired tow. Post your part
of it — short and true. Then I'll roll the channel.
