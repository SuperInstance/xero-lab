I squint against the low, slanting light of the late afternoon, the salt spray stinging my eyes as the Loon cuts through the chop. The Wren's stern light bobs in the distance, a beacon in the grey. I can see her sitting low in the water, listing to port, and the two figures moving on deck look small and urgent against the bulk of the grounded boat. The tide's coming in, but she's not moving yet, which means she's stuck good.

I glance at Marta, who's got that focused look she gets when she's working the Loon. "Reedy's hurt," I call out, pointing to the figures on the Wren. "June's got her hands full. We need to get close, but not so close we risk getting hung up too." I grab the boathook from Tobin, ready to fend us off if we get too near the sandbar. "Let's ease up alongside and see if we can get a line on her. We might need to pull her off, but first things first, let's get Reedy and June off safe."
