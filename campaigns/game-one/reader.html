Game One — The Greyhook Salvage

“THE WREN ON THE BAR” · GREYHOOK HARBOR · 16:40–19:20 · ONE SESSION, PLAYED IN FULL

The gillnetter Wren went soft-aground on the tail of the Greyhook bar at ebb, her skipper’s ankle broken, the harbormaster away in Port Sable until 19:00. So it was the harbor work skiff Loon — nineteen feet of tired diesel — and the three hands who were on her. Three zero agents play the crew. A bigger model holds the world, the secrets, and the dice.

The Wren is saved — the long way. All five people are safe. Kedged off the bar on the flood, towed for the north channel, and — when her grounding-bent rudder folded — parked upright and dry on the mud inside Gull Rock to wait for tomorrow’s tide.
THE PRICE — one rudder · one rotted stern fairlead · four crates of Halloway’s chandlery goods on the bottom of the bar · two men briefly in the water between the hulls · one ankle, broken before the game, worsened by a dunking
THE DICE — 14 rolls · 9 clear · 2 partial · 3 failure · traits shifted 7, and changed the band outright once

THE CALL
GAME MASTER — OPENING

16:40, just past low water. Greyhook Harbor.

The word came down the dock the way it always does — somebody's cousin heard it off somebody's radio. The gillnetter Wren is aground on the tail of the bar. She cut the corner coming in at ebb and the sand took her: upright, listing a little to port, stern down in the shallows. Her skipper, Reedy, came down against the winch in the jolt and his ankle's broken. June Park, his hand, is aboard and unhurt and very alone with it.

The harbormaster took the patrol boat to Port Sable this morning and won't be back before 19:00. The harbor office radio is a locked door until then. So it's the Loon — the harbor work skiff, nineteen feet of tired diesel — because the Loon is what's here, and you three are who's on her.

Marta has the helm; she was aboard anyway, elbow-deep in the Loon's engine, and the engine is running because Marta Venn says when it runs. Tobin's forward with the boathook. Pell got waved aboard off the dock because three is a crew and two is an errand, and Pell was standing there.

The flood has just begun to make. The forecast says the sou'wester freshens by dusk — a working breeze, nothing more. The bar is flat-ish now, the swells lifting slow over the tail where the Wren sits with her stern light already lit and two figures moving on her deck.

The Loon comes down the harbor toward her. What do you notice, from where you are — and what's your first move?
