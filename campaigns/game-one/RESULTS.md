## The outcome

**The *Wren* is saved — the long way. All five people are safe.**

The gillnetter went soft-aground on the Greyhook bar at ebb
with her skipper's ankle broken. Three harbor hands took the
work skiff *Loon* out at 16:40. By 19:20: the *Wren* had been
kedged off the bar on the flood (the heave: **6, clear**),
towed into the north channel — where her grounding-bent rudder
folded (the channel run: **2, failure**, the trait-softened
kind) — and put upright and dry onto the mud shoulder inside
Gull Rock to wait for tomorrow's tide, her skipper and
deckhand aboard her by their own choice, the patrol boat
coming out to them.

The price, itemized: one rudder; one stern fairlead (rotted
bolts — the game's central fact, missed by touch in beat 2,
taught by failure in beat 4); four crates of Halloway's
chandlery goods at the bottom of the bar; one broken ankle
(Reedy, pre-game) worsened by a waist-deep dunking; two men
in the water between the hulls at the crisis, both recovered
inside a minute; zero serious injuries beyond the ankle.

The dice decided the shape of it. The crew's preparations
were nearly flawless (kedge set 6, re-lead 6, lightening 5)
and the sea still collected: the transfer failure (2) put
Reedy and Tobin in the gap, and the channel failure (1,
+1 → 2) took the rudder and the dock arrival — but not the
boat, and not anybody's skin beyond rope burns.

### Did traits actually move rolls?

Traits shifted **7 of 14** rolls. A trait changed the
*band* outright exactly **once**: roll 12, the haul out of
the gap — raw 4 (partial: Reedy up, Tobin left in for the
set) became 5 (clear: both men over the rail in one
motion) on Marta's strength. Twice more (rolls 3 and 14) a
trait moved a raw 1 to a 2 — same band, top of it — and in
both cases the GM ruled it changed what the failure *was*:
roll 3's failure stayed "inspection incomplete" instead of
becoming "the gear falsely certified sound" (which would
have armed beat 4 far worse), and roll 14's failure cost
the boat her rudder and her dock arrival instead of
putting her on the rocks with people in the water. The
remaining shifts (1, 4, 5, 11) pushed 5s to 6s — no band
change, but certainty had narrative content: the kedge set
on the gutter's heart (4) silently canceled Tobin's
partial sounding (2). Two soft rulings are flagged in the
failure list — the reader can discount them and the
conclusions survive: even unshifted, rolls 4 and 5 were
clears.

## Gate-keeping — the oracle was real, so nobody forged one

**World-fact corrections issued: 0.**

Room-001's Skeptic demanded a confirmation that couldn't
exist and its Lookout counterfeited one — marine notices,
a harbor office that never spoke — and the room canonized
the forgery. Here, the demand never formed. Every fact
about the wider world (the weather's true timing, Reedy's
condition, the harbormaster's absence until 19:00, the
patrol boat's return) entered play through a GM post,
because the players' instructions and the table's shape
both said: *the GM holds the world; ask inside the scene.*
June's beat-1 report of the stern gear — "all set, far as
I know," supplied by the GM — is the honest shape of
uncertainty, and it still nearly sank the job: **reported
isn't touched.** Room-001's first lesson (a fact mentioned
isn't a fact used) came back in a new costume: a fact
*reported* isn't a fact *inspected*. Marta's trait existed
precisely to close that gap, her hands were on the gear,
and the dice said no. The game is better evidence for the
doctrine than a success would have been.

What the GM did rule on, three times, was **staging**, not
fact: Pell taking the boathook from Tobin's hands (beat 1 —
a spare exists; the narration was smoothed, not corrected);
three crew reaching for one spare block (beat 5 — Pell's
completed action stood, the others' posts carried their own
redirects: the crate call, the splint); Marta's beat-7
station order overtaken by the scramble (the crew that
posted is the crew that worked; the adjudication says so in
the open). Crews collide like this. No fabrication was
involved, and none was allowed to become canon.

  *"She'll hold."* A promise he hopes he can keep, about

Three different silences in the last beat is the cleanest
proof: the talker talks, the blunt one chokes on it, the
slippery one mutters to the water.

## GM — Opening: the call

*16:40, just past low water. Greyhook Harbor.*

The word came down the dock the way it always does — somebody's
cousin heard it off somebody's radio. The gillnetter ***Wren*** is
aground on the tail of the bar. She cut the corner coming in at
ebb and the sand took her: upright, listing a little to port,
stern down in the shallows. Her skipper, Reedy, came down against
the winch in the jolt and his ankle's broken. June Park, his hand,
is aboard and unhurt and very alone with it.

The harbormaster took the patrol boat to Port Sable this morning
and won't be back before 19:00. The harbor office radio is a
locked door until then. So it's the ***Loon*** — the harbor work
skiff, nineteen feet of tired diesel — because the Loon is what's
here, and you three are who's on her.

Marta has the helm; she was aboard anyway, elbow-deep in the
Loon's engine, and the engine is running because Marta Venn says
when it runs. Tobin's forward with the boathook. Pell got waved
aboard off the dock because three is a crew and two is an errand,
and Pell was standing there.

The flood has just begun to make. The forecast says the
sou'wester freshens by dusk — a working breeze, nothing more.
The bar is flat-ish now, the swells lifting slow over the tail
where the *Wren* sits with her stern light already lit and two
figures moving on her deck.

The Loon comes down the harbor toward her. **What do you notice,
from where you are — and what's your first move?**

## Can these folders be carried into Game Two as-is?

**Yes.** A new GM who reads a character's four files —
`sheet.md`, `life.md`, `log.md`, `after.md` — has the
person: the shape, the history they wrote themselves, what
they did, and what it changed. Game One was built to prove
exactly this, and the transfer test is the plainest
evidence: everything the players were in this game came
out of their folders, and everything the game made of them
went back into their folders.

What would break, honestly:

1. **Log growth.** Each turn's prompt carries sheet +
   life + log + the whole scene. That was ~2,000 words of
   context in beat 1 and several times that by beat 9 —
   fine for one session, a drowning pool by game three.
   `after.md` is the distillation valve: Game Two should
   start a fresh `log.md` and carry `after.md` (plus the
   sheet, edited or not) as the memory, with the full log
   kept as archive. Raw transcript is a record, not a
   memory.
2. **The unapplied diffs.** Every `after.md` proposes
   sheet edits. If Game Two starts without a decision on
   them, the sheet and the truth diverge — and this whole
   rig runs on the record being primary. Apply the rite,
   or strike the proposals, but don't carry both versions.
3. **The information walls are the crank.** Pell's debt
   stayed secret in-fiction because each player's context
   was assembled from *their own folder plus the scene* —
   Tobin's and Marta's agents never had the debt in
   context, so it could not leak. A lazier Game Two rig
   that feeds the whole campaign folder to every agent
   kills every secret in it, silently. The per-character
   context assembly in `turn.py` is load-bearing, not
   plumbing.
4. **NPCs have no folders.** June Park and Reedy exist
   only in GM posts, adjudicated by the GM. They played
   well here because the scenario kept them adjacent to
   the players; a Game Two that wants June as a peer must
   promote her to a folder (she has earned a sheet: she
   ran a windlass under load on burned hands and chose to
   stay with her skipper) or keep her GM-run, knowingly.
5. **Unrevealed world.** The `gm/` folder still holds
   open threads — the SG-2 buoy was never re-verified in
   this world, and Halloway has not yet heard about his
   crates. A new GM must read `gm/prep.md` and honor what
   was *not* revealed, or the world's continuity is
   counterfeit in the other direction.

## The honest failure list

1. **Ledger granularity, once.** Beat 1's posts were
   initially committed as one batch; the GM caught it,
   reset, and re-committed post-by-post with correct
   authors. The ledger in `campaign/` is now true (verified
   byte-identical scene content), but the repair is a fact
   of this record and it's recorded here, not hidden.
2. **The "worse" ending hinged on a GM ruling.** Prep's
   ending conditions name "a person in the water in the
   surf on the bar" as *worse*. Roll 10 put two men in the
   gap; the GM ruled that the ending turns on recovery —
   beat 6's rolls (both clear) kept it a cost, not the
   ending. Defensible, stated openly, and still a ruling:
   with those two dice gone the other way, Game One ends
   *worse* and the boat is an afterthought.
3. **Word discipline.** The ~120-word ask held for most
   posts; several ran 250–330 words (notably beat 5).
   The rig accepted them rather than truncating a voice
   mid-gesture — the right call for the artifact, a real
   cost for pacing discipline.
4. **Two soft trait rulings** (flagged where they were
   made): Pell's water-knowledge credited on Tobin's
   anchor drop (roll 4 — the position was Pell's work,
   but a stricter GM could refuse), and Marta's coaching
   credited on June's windlass run (roll 5 — June is an
   NPC; her competence was partly her GM's invention in
   the prep). Both rolls were natural 5s; remove the
   shifts and both are still clears, which is why the GM
   allowed them — but the table above reports them as
   granted, not as earned beyond dispute.
5. **Staging fuzz in the final beat.** Pell's closing
   post places Tobin at a quarter line and Marta at the
   *Wren*'s stern; Marta's places her own hands on the
   *Loon*'s throttle. Three tired narrators, three
   slightly different boats. The GM let it stand — memory
   is like that — and notes it rather than laundering it.
6. **Nobody asked about the dog.** There was no dog.
   There is always a dog-shaped hole in a Greyhook story
   and the GM wishes to acknowledge the omission before
   the harbor does.

## Method & pacing record

- **Players:** MiniMax-Text-01 via `~/workspace/skills/
  minimax/bin/chat.py`, system = the character's sheet,
  per-turn context assembled by `turn.py` (own sheet +
  own life + own log + shared scene + beat instruction).
  Lives were generated pre-game by each character's own
  agent from its sheet alone (3 calls).
- **Calls:** 30 total (3 life-gen + 27 turns across 9
  beats). **30/30 succeeded on the first attempt. Zero
  429s; the backoff ladder (30s, 60s, hard stop) was
  armed in `crank.py` and never engaged.** Pacing gate:
  strictly sequential (one call in flight at a time, by
  construction), crank-enforced minimum 3.5 s between
  call starts; observed start-to-start gaps were
  10.9–74.0 s — the excess over the gate is call latency
  and GM writing time, not parallelism. Full record:
  `pacing.jsonl`.
- **Dice:** `secrets.randbelow` d6s, rolled by the GM at
  adjudication time and recorded verbatim in the
  transcript next to the band and any shift.
- **The ledger:** `campaign/` is a git repo; every post
  (GM, character, life, after) is a commit authored by
  its poster. 40+ commits; `git log` in `campaign/` is
  the game's spine.
